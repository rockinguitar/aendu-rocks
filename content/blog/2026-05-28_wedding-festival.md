When you invite people from two countries to celebrate with you, one day isn't enough. So ours turned into five.

**Thursday** started gently — dinner out with my father, my stepmother, and our friend Chris. A quiet warm-up before the apartment filled up.

**Friday**, we fired up the grill at home. Family and friends from Switzerland arrived, and suddenly the apartment was full of languages, food, and laughter.

**Saturday** was the big one. The day started badly — rain and wind, the kind that makes you fear the worst. But half an hour before the aperitif, the clouds broke and the sun came through. We were incredibly lucky.

We started with drinks on a rooftop, followed by a three-course dinner at Kampen Bistro. Many great speeches were given — Norwegians love speeches, and that day proved it. Then we returned home for the after-party.

**Sunday** slowed down a notch: a relaxed dinner out with some of the Swiss guys. 

**Monday**, the barbecue grill was lit again at home. By **Tuesday morning**, the last guests were gone.

The best feedback I got was from my Norwegian friends. They only met my Swiss friends for a single day, but they were so fond of them that they would love to meet them again. Hearing that they liked my friends so much meant more to me than I expected.

The whole celebration felt like surfing on a high. I was running on full throttle for five straight days, and by the end I was very, very tired. But when the last guest left, an emptiness set in. Everything we had planned and looked forward to for so long was simply… over. A bit sad, but mostly grateful.
