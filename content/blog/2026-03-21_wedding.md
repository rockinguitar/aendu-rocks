Oslo was covered in fog and rain for days before the wedding, but on 20 March the sun came out. After more than 10 years together, Ingunn and I got married in the beautiful Oslo city hall.

Our friends invited themselves to the ceremony. Instead of being 4, we were 10 people meeting inside the city hall.

![André and Ingunn at their wedding at City Hall](/img/events/wedding-city-hall.webp)

The city hall is marvellous — I cannot imagine a better place to get married. From the outside it is quite spectacular: two towers, and as you go up to the entrance, carved wooden paintings on the left and right telling Norse mythology, together known as the Yggdrasil frieze. The wedding ceremony was upstairs in the Munch Room, where Edvard Munch's *Livet* hangs on the wall. Even the halls downstairs are covered with enormous murals and frescoes telling the story of Norway. I don't know if any church can match the city hall.

## The ceremony

Ingunn looked stunning in her bright wedding dress and silver scarf.

There were no vows. Our officiant was a warm woman with good karma. She read the promise, and I think it suits us very well. My voice was scrambled because I was so touched, so my "yes" was almost not audible.

{{< youtube id="sf6H99sWxOk" class="vertical-video" />}}

Then we exchanged the rings, which have *Mo anam cara* engraved on them, and also the name of the partner. It means "soul friend". We were inspired by our favorite bar Anam Cara in Oslo. We have been there several times, and we love their cocktails and the vibe.

## After the ceremony

From the city hall, we made our way to the bar Anam Cara, which is owned by two of our friends, Thomas and Hussain. They had opened it early, just for us. We had an aperitif there, and Hussain played the wedding march when we wheeled inside. What a great idea!

After Anam Cara we had a delicious 3-course dinner at Rivoli in Bjørvika. Everything tasted excellent, and the service was superb.

It was an unforgettable day, and we were so lucky to celebrate it with our friends.
